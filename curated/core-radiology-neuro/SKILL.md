# Core Radiology – Neuroimaging

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Ellen X. Sun, Junzi Shi, Jacob C. Mandell), Cambridge University Press, 2021. Chapters 10–12 (Brain, Head & Neck, Spine).

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks for residents. Working toolkit for stroke, brain masses, trauma, infection, and spine emergencies.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Stroke – Ischemic
**Time is brain.** Noncontrast CT first to exclude hemorrhage, then CTA ± CTP or MRI with DWI.

**Early CT signs of MCA infarct**
- Hyperdense MCA sign
- Insular ribbon sign (loss of gray-white differentiation)
- Obscuration of lentiform nucleus
- Sulcal effacement

**DWI sequence is the most sensitive for acute ischemia.**  
ADC correlates with restricted diffusion (dark on ADC = true restriction).

**Arterial territories** (must know)
- ACA: medial frontal/parietal
- MCA: lateral frontal, temporal, parietal; lenticulostriates
- PCA: occipital, medial temporal, thalamus
- Anterior choroidal, Heubner, etc. for deep structures

### Stroke – Hemorrhagic
**Location guides etiology**
- Basal ganglia / thalamus / cerebellum → hypertensive
- Lobar (peripheral) → amyloid angiopathy (elderly) or AVM/tumor (younger)
- Extra-axial (subdural, epidural, SAH) → trauma or aneurysm

**CTA spot sign** = active extravasation → higher risk of hematoma expansion.

### Extra-axial vs Intra-axial Mass
**Extra-axial clues**
- CSF cleft
- Cortical buckling / gray matter interposed
- Broad dural base / dural tail
- Bone hyperostosis or erosion
- Displaced vessels

**Intra-axial** → surrounded by parenchyma, expands cortex or white matter.

## 2. Brain Tumor Frameworks (Post-2016 WHO)

### Diffuse Gliomas – Molecular Era
- **IDH-wildtype** → usually glioblastoma (GBM), worse prognosis, elderly, de novo
- **IDH-mutant + 1p/19q co-deleted** → oligodendroglioma (better prognosis)
- **IDH-mutant + 1p/19q non-co-deleted** → astrocytoma

**Imaging clues**
- GBM: heterogeneous, irregular enhancement, central necrosis, elevated CBV, surrounding T2/FLAIR
- Lower-grade: less enhancement, more infiltrative, slower growth

### Classic Cystic Mass with Mural Nodule
- Child / cerebellar → pilocytic astrocytoma
- Young adult / cerebellar + VHL → hemangioblastoma
- Infant / supratentorial cortical → desmoplastic infantile ganglioglioma/astrocytoma

### Meningioma
- Most common extra-axial and benign primary intracranial tumor
- Dural tail, hyperostosis, homogeneous enhancement
- Can be multiple in NF2

### Metastasis
- Most common intra-axial mass in adults overall
- Multiple, at gray-white junction, variable enhancement/necrosis
- Primary search: lung, breast, melanoma, renal, colorectal

## 3. Infection & Inflammation High-Yield

| Entity                    | Key Imaging Features                          | Notes |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|-------|
| **Pyogenic abscess**      | Ring-enhancing, restricted diffusion in core, thin regular wall | Surgical emergency if large |
| **Toxoplasmosis**         | Multiple ring-enhancing, basal ganglia/thalami, AIDS | vs lymphoma (more solid, periventricular) |
| **PML**                   | Asymmetric T2/FLAIR white matter, no enhancement, no mass effect | JC virus, AIDS |
| **HSV encephalitis**      | Temporal/insular, asymmetric, restricted diffusion, hemorrhage | Emergency – start acyclovir |
| **TB**                    | Basal meningitis, hydrocephalus, infarcts, tuberculomas | |

## 4. Trauma Frameworks

**Epidural** – lens-shaped, does not cross sutures, arterial (middle meningeal), lucid interval possible  
**Subdural** – crescentic, crosses sutures, venous, can be acute/subacute/chronic  
**SAH** – traumatic (superficial) vs aneurysmal (basal cisterns)  
**Diffuse axonal injury** – shear injury at gray-white, corpus callosum, brainstem; best seen on GRE/SWI and DWI  
**Contusion** – coup-contrecoup, hemorrhagic, inferior frontal and temporal tips classic

## 5. Spine Essentials (High-Yield)

**Cord compression / cauda equina** – emergency; look for mass, disc, abscess, hematoma  
**Epidural abscess** – rim-enhancing collection, often with discitis/osteomyelitis  
**Transverse myelitis vs infarct** – longer segment vs vascular territory (anterior spinal artery)  
**Spondylodiscitis** – endplate destruction, disc T2 hyperintensity, enhancement, paraspinal/epidural phlegmon or abscess  

**Disc herniation nomenclature**  
- Protrusion vs extrusion vs sequestration  
- Central, subarticular, foraminal, extraforaminal

## 6. Classic Signs & Patterns

| Sign / Pattern              | Meaning                                      | Importance |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------|
| **Hyperdense MCA**          | Acute thrombus                               | Early infarct sign |
| **Insular ribbon**          | Loss of gray-white in insula                 | Early MCA infarct |
| **Dural tail**              | Meningioma (or other extra-axial)            | Extra-axial clue |
| **Light-bulb T2**           | Pheochromocytoma (or other)                  | Intense T2 |
| **Target sign (spine)**     | Cord infarct or syringohydromyelia           | Central gray matter |
| **Empty delta sign**        | Superior sagittal sinus thrombosis           | Filling defect |

## 7. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Do not call every ring-enhancing lesion an abscess — check diffusion, clinical context, and multiplicity.
- IDH status now supersedes pure histology for glioma prognosis and classification.
- Lobar hemorrhage in elderly → think amyloid first; in young → vascular malformation or tumor.
- Subdural can be acute on chronic — look for mixed density and membranes.
- “Mass effect out of proportion to enhancement” often points to lymphoma or PML rather than typical abscess.
- Always evaluate for cord compression on any spine MRI ordered for back pain with neurologic symptoms.
