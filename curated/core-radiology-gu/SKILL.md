# Core Radiology – Genitourinary Imaging

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Ellen X. Sun, Junzi Shi, Jacob C. Mandell), Cambridge University Press, 2021. Chapter 3 – Genitourinary Imaging.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks for residents. Working toolkit for adrenal incidentalomas, cystic renal masses, solid renal masses, stones, and obstruction.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Adrenal Incidentaloma Algorithm
1. **Noncontrast CT ≤10 HU** → lipid-rich adenoma (stop; ~70–80% of adenomas)
2. **>10 HU** → perform adrenal protocol CT (noncontrast + portal venous + 15-min delayed)
3. Calculate washout:
   - Absolute washout = (Enhanced – Delayed) / (Enhanced – Unenhanced) × 100
   - Relative washout = (Enhanced – Delayed) / Enhanced × 100
4. **Absolute >60% or Relative >40%** → adenoma
5. Does not meet criteria → indeterminate (further workup based on size, history of malignancy, prior imaging)

### Enhancement Thresholds (Solid Renal Mass)
**CT**  
- <10 HU: no enhancement  
- 10–19 HU: equivocal  
- ≥20 HU: definite enhancement  

**MRI**  
- <15%: no enhancement  
- 15–19%: equivocal  
- ≥20%: definite enhancement  

Lesions smaller than twice the slice thickness are “too small to characterize.”

### Hydronephrosis Framework
**Obstructive (most common)**  
- Calculus (by far #1)  
- Ureteral / bladder malignancy  
- External compression (mass, retroperitoneal fibrosis)  

**Non-obstructive**  
- Vesicoureteral reflux  
- Pregnancy (right > left)

## 2. Bosniak Classification of Cystic Renal Masses (2019 Update)

| Category | Key Features | Malignancy Risk | Management |
|----------|--------------|-----------------|------------|
| **I** | Simple cyst: thin (≤2 mm) smooth wall, may enhance; no septa or calcifications | <1% | None |
| **II** | Thin septa (≤2 mm, <4), nonenhancing; or homogeneous hyperdense (≥70 HU) / T1 hyperintense ≤3 cm; or homogeneous low-attenuation too small to characterize | <1% | None |
| **IIF** | Minimally complicated: more septa, mildly thickened walls/septa, perceived enhancement, or thick/nodular calcification | 0–38% (usually low) | Imaging follow-up |
| **III** | Thickened walls or septa with measurable enhancement; or multilocular cystic | ~50% | Surgery or close follow-up (shared decision) |
| **IV** | Enhancing soft-tissue nodule independent of wall/septum | ~90% | Surgery (unless significant comorbidities) |

**MRI advantages**: better for characterizing enhancement and distinguishing IIF vs III; not limited by calcification.

## 3. Solid Renal Masses – High-Yield

**Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC)**
- Clear cell: most common, hypervascular, often has microscopic fat or necrosis
- Papillary: hypovascular, can be multifocal, T2 hypointense
- Chromophobe: intermediate features
- Cystic RCC can fall into Bosniak III/IV

**Angiomyolipoma (AML)**
- Classic: macroscopic fat (almost pathognomonic)
- Minimal-fat AML: hyperdense on noncontrast CT, T2 hypointense, can mimic RCC → biopsy often needed
- Risk of hemorrhage increases with size (>4 cm traditionally)

**Oncocytoma**
- Benign; central scar classic but not specific (also seen in chromophobe RCC)
- Cannot be reliably distinguished from RCC on imaging alone

**Indications for renal mass biopsy** (after full imaging workup)
- Distinguish RCC from metastasis
- Infection vs cystic neoplasm
- Hyperdense homogeneously enhancing mass (minimal-fat AML vs RCC)
- High surgical risk patient
- Multiple/bilateral masses
- Solitary or transplant kidney
- Pre-ablation tissue diagnosis

## 4. Adrenal Lesions Beyond Adenoma

- **Myelolipoma**: macroscopic fat + soft tissue; benign
- **Pheochromocytoma**: avidly enhancing, T2 “light-bulb,” can have hemorrhage/cystic change; never biopsy without biochemical workup
- **Adrenal cortical carcinoma**: large (>4–6 cm), heterogeneous, necrosis, invasion, hormone production
- **Metastasis**: often bilateral; history of primary is key
- **Hemorrhage**: anticoagulation, trauma, stress; Waterhouse-Friderichsen (meningococcemia)

## 5. Urolithiasis & Obstruction

**Stone composition & visibility**
- Calcium stones (most common) → radiopaque on XR and CT
- Uric acid, xanthine, matrix, pure struvite, indinavir → radiolucent on XR; most still dense on CT except indinavir and matrix

**Soft-tissue rim sign**: soft tissue surrounding a calcification → favors ureteral stone over phlebolith

**Dual-energy CT**: can help determine stone composition for management decisions

## 6. Classic Signs & Patterns

| Sign / Pattern              | Meaning                                      | Importance |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------|
| **Soft-tissue rim sign**    | Edematous ureter around stone                | Distinguishes stone from phlebolith |
| **Bear-paw sign**           | Xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis           | Chronic infection with lipid-laden macrophages |
| **Putty kidney**            | End-stage TB                                 | Autonephrectomy |
| **Cortical rim sign**       | Preserved peripheral enhancement in infarct  | Collateral capsular flow |
| **Faceless kidney**         | Loss of corticomedullary differentiation     | Acute pyelonephritis or lymphoma |

## 7. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Do not call every hyperdense renal lesion a hemorrhagic cyst — check enhancement carefully.
- Lipid-poor adenoma still exists; washout criteria are required when HU >10.
- Minimal-fat AML can perfectly mimic RCC — know the biopsy indications.
- Bosniak IIF needs follow-up; do not dismiss as “probably benign forever.”
- Indinavir stones are invisible on CT — use ultrasound if clinical suspicion is high in HIV patients.
- Never biopsy a suspected pheochromocytoma without biochemical confirmation.
- “Too small to characterize” is a real category — do not force a Bosniak or enhancement call.
