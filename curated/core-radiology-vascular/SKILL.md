# Core Radiology – Vascular Imaging

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Sun, Shi, Mandell), Chapter 8 – Vascular Imaging.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks for residents. Working toolkit for acute aortic syndrome, PE, aneurysms, and mesenteric/peripheral vascular disease.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Acute Aortic Syndrome
**Components**
- Aortic dissection
- Intramural hematoma (IMH)
- Penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer (PAU)
- Traumatic aortic injury / rupture

**Stanford Classification (most used)**
- **Type A**: involves ascending aorta → surgical emergency
- **Type B**: starts distal to left subclavian → usually medical management ± endovascular

**Key imaging signs**
- Intimal flap
- Double-barrel lumen (true vs false lumen)
- Intramural high-attenuation hematoma on noncontrast CT
- Ulcer-like projection
- Periaortic stranding or hemothorax/hemopericardium (rupture)

### Pulmonary Embolism
- CTPA is first-line in most adults
- Look for filling defects in pulmonary arteries, RV strain (RV/LV ratio >1, septal bowing), pulmonary infarcts (Hampton hump)
- Chronic PE: webs, stenoses, mosaic attenuation, enlarged PA

### Aneurysm vs Pseudoaneurysm
- True aneurysm: all three wall layers, often atherosclerotic or connective tissue
- Pseudoaneurysm: contained rupture, risk of free rupture higher
- Size thresholds for intervention vary by location (abdominal aorta typically ≥5.5 cm)

---

## 2. Mesenteric & Visceral

**Acute mesenteric ischemia**
- Arterial (embolus or thrombosis) – most common
- Venous thrombosis
- Non-occlusive (low flow)
- CTA findings: vessel occlusion, bowel wall thickening or thinning, pneumatosis, portal venous gas, solid organ infarcts

**Median arcuate ligament syndrome**
- Dynamic compression of celiac axis; often incidental

---

## 3. Peripheral & Other

- Peripheral arterial disease: multilevel atherosclerotic stenoses/occlusions
- Popliteal entrapment, cystic adventitial disease (younger patients)
- Deep vein thrombosis: ultrasound first-line; CT/MR venography for central veins
- May-Thurner: left common iliac vein compression by right common iliac artery

---

## 4. Classic Signs & Patterns

| Sign / Pattern              | Meaning                                      | Importance |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------|
| **Intimal flap**            | Dissection                                   | Diagnostic |
| **Crescent sign**           | Intramural hematoma or impending rupture     | High risk |
| **Hampton hump**            | Pulmonary infarct                            | Peripheral PE |
| **Westermark sign**         | Oligemia distal to PE                        | Classic but insensitive |
| **Beak sign**               | Acute angle of false lumen                   | Helps identify false lumen |
| **Cobweb sign**             | Residual strands in false lumen              | Dissection |

---

## 5. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Type A dissection is a surgical emergency even if the patient looks stable.
- Intramural hematoma can progress to frank dissection or rupture — treat as acute aortic syndrome.
- Do not dismiss a “small” PE if there is significant RV strain.
- Pulsation artifact in the ascending aorta can mimic a flap — use ECG-gating or careful multiplanar review.
- Chronic PE can look like acute if you do not recognize webs and stenoses.
