# Core Radiology – Thoracic Imaging

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Ellen X. Sun, Junzi Shi, Jacob C. Mandell), Cambridge University Press, 2021. Chapter 1 – Thoracic Imaging.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks and pattern recognition tools for residents and radiologists. Not a summary — a working toolkit.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Secondary Pulmonary Lobule (SPL) – The Fundamental Unit
- Size: 1–2.5 cm
- Center: centrilobular artery + bronchiole
- Periphery: interlobular septa containing veins + lymphatics
- Everything in lung disease pattern recognition is interpreted relative to the SPL

### Approach to Multiple Micronodules (Decision Tree)
1. Are there **subpleural nodules**?
   - **No** → Centrilobular (never touch pleura)
   - **Yes** → Go to step 2
2. Are nodules along bronchovascular bundles + septa in a **non-uniform** distribution?
   - Yes → **Perilymphatic**
   - No (uniform/random) → **Random** (hematogenous)

| Distribution     | Key Feature                          | Classic Causes                                      |
|------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Centrilobular    | Never touch pleura                   | HP, viral, aspiration, metastatic calcification     |
| Tree-in-bud      | Branching + budding nodules          | Infectious bronchiolitis (TB, atypical, viral, asp) |
| Perilymphatic    | Subpleural + peribronchovascular + septal | Sarcoid, pneumoconiosis, lymphangitic carcinomatosis |
| Random           | Random, often touch pleura           | Hematogenous mets, miliary TB/fungal                |

### Atelectasis Mechanisms
- **Obstructive**: resorption of air distal to blocked bronchus (mucus, tumor, foreign body)
- **Relaxation (passive)**: adjacent mass effect (effusion, pneumothorax, mass)
- **Adhesive**: surfactant deficiency (neonatal RDS, ARDS)
- **Cicatricial**: fibrosis pulling lung down

### Direct vs Indirect Signs of Atelectasis
**Direct**: fissure displacement, plate-like/triangular opacity, vascular crowding  
**Indirect**: diaphragmatic elevation, rib crowding, mediastinal shift toward side of loss, compensatory overinflation, hilar displacement

## 2. High-Yield Classic Signs (Must-Know for Core)

| Sign                  | Meaning / Appearance                                      | Associated Finding                  |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **Luftsichel**        | Crescent of air lateral to aortic arch                    | Left upper lobe collapse            |
| **Golden S (reverse S)** | Convex central mass + concave lateral fissure           | Right upper lobe collapse (mass)    |
| **Comet tail**        | Curved vessels/bronchi swirling into opacity              | Round atelectasis                   |
| **Flat waist**        | Flattening of left heart border                           | Left lower lobe collapse            |
| **Juxtaphrenic peak** | Triangular opacity at diaphragm                           | Upper lobe volume loss              |
| **Bulging fissure**   | Fissure pushed outward by voluminous exudate              | Klebsiella pneumonia                |
| **Galaxy sign**       | Confluent nodules surrounding a central lesion            | Sarcoidosis                         |

## 3. Lobar Atelectasis Patterns (Frontal + Lateral)

**Left Upper Lobe**
- Frontal: veil-like opacity, obscured left heart border
- Lateral: anterior displacement of major fissure
- Classic: Luftsichel sign

**Right Upper Lobe**
- Frontal: superior displacement of minor fissure ± Golden S sign
- Lateral: wedge-shaped opacity superiorly
- Always consider central obstructing mass in adults

**Left Lower Lobe**
- Frontal: triangular retrocardiac opacity
- Lateral: posterior hazy opacity
- Flat waist sign

**Right Lower Lobe**
- Mirror image of LLL
- Triangular opacity with apex toward hilum, medial hemidiaphragm obscured
- Right heart border preserved

**Right Middle Lobe**
- Frontal: often subtle – silhouetting of right heart border
- Lateral: triangular opacity over the heart
- Combined RML + RLL = bronchus intermedius obstruction

**Round Atelectasis – All 5 criteria required**
1. Adjacent pleural abnormality
2. Opacity contacts the pleura
3. Round or elliptical shape
4. Volume loss in the lobe
5. Comet tail sign

## 4. Consolidation vs Ground-Glass Opacification (GGO)

**Consolidation**
- Complete alveolar filling (blood, pus, water, cells)
- Vessels obscured on unenhanced CT
- Air bronchograms common
- Acute DDx: pneumonia >> aspiration, hemorrhage, ARDS, severe edema
- Chronic DDx: adenocarcinoma, lymphoma, organizing pneumonia, chronic eosinophilic pneumonia

**Ground-Glass**
- Partial filling / wall thickening / incomplete aeration
- Vessels still visible
- Acute DDx: edema, atypical/viral/PCP pneumonia, hemorrhage, ARDS
- Chronic DDx: adenocarcinoma, OP, CEP, DIP/NSIP/HP

**Peripheral GGO or Consolidation**
- Organizing pneumonia
- Chronic eosinophilic pneumonia (upper lobe)
- Pulmonary infarction

## 5. Interlobular Septal Thickening & Crazy Paving

**Smooth** → pulmonary edema (by far #1), lymphangitic carcinomatosis  
**Nodular / irregular / asymmetric** → lymphangitic carcinomatosis, sarcoidosis

**Crazy Paving** = GGO + interlobular septal thickening  
Classic associations: pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (original description), edema, hemorrhage, ARDS, PCP, adenocarcinoma, lipoid pneumonia

## 6. Cavitary & Cystic Disease

**Solitary cavity**
- Wall ≤4 mm → usually benign
- Wall >15 mm or spiculated → concerning for malignancy
- Top DDx: squamous cell ca, TB, fungal, bacterial

**Multiple cavities**
- Septic emboli (peripheral)
- GPA (vasculitis)
- Metastases (classically squamous)

**Cysts (thin-walled)**
- LAM (diffuse, uniform, chylous effusion)
- PLCH (irregular, upper lobe, nodules)
- LIP (Sjögren)
- Birt-Hogg-Dubé
- Late PCP

Distinguish from emphysema (no walls, may contain central vessels).

---

## 7. Fibrosis Distribution

**Basal-predominant**
- UIP / IPF (honeycombing almost always present)
- NSIP (more ground glass, less honeycombing)
- Connective tissue disease, asbestosis

**Upper-lobe predominant**
- End-stage sarcoidosis
- Chronic hypersensitivity pneumonitis
- End-stage silicosis / coal-worker’s

---

## 8. Pulmonary Infection – Practical Classification

**Community-acquired**: S. pneumoniae #1; atypical (Mycoplasma, viral, Chlamydia) in young healthy; Legionella in elderly smokers; Klebsiella in alcoholics (bulging fissure)

**Hospital / Healthcare-associated / Ventilator**: MRSA, resistant gram-negatives (Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter)

**Immunocompromised**: add PCP, Aspergillus, Nocardia, CMV, etc. Neutropenia → fungal risk

**Radiographic patterns**
- Lobar: typical bacterial
- Bronchopneumonia: patchy, multi-lobar
- Interstitial (CXR term ≈ GGO on CT): atypical / viral / PCP
- Round pneumonia: children, S. pneumoniae

---

## 9. Anti-Patterns & Pitfalls (Core Exam Traps)

- Do **not** call left upper lobe collapse “pneumonia” or “effusion” — look for luftsichel and anterior major fissure.
- Golden S sign = mass until proven otherwise.
- Round atelectasis requires **all five** criteria; incomplete = do not call it.
- Centrilobular nodules **never** touch the pleura — if they do, rethink the distribution.
- Smooth septal thickening is edema until proven otherwise; nodular is lymphatic process.
- Upper lobe fibrosis is **not** IPF.
