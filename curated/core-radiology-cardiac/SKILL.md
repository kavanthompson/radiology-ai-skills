# Core Radiology – Cardiac Imaging

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Ellen X. Sun, Junzi Shi, Jacob C. Mandell), Cambridge University Press, 2021. Chapter 7 – Cardiac Imaging.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks for residents. Working toolkit for coronary CTA, cardiac MRI, cardiomyopathy, and chest radiograph cardiac silhouette analysis.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Cardiac Silhouette Analysis (Chest Radiograph)
**Enlarged cardiac silhouette**
- Evaluate left atrium and aorta:
  - Enlarged LA → mitral regurgitation (or mixed)
  - Enlarged aorta → aortic regurgitation
  - Neither enlarged → pericardial effusion, dilated cardiomyopathy, high-output states, etc.

**Normal-sized cardiac silhouette**
- Enlarged LA → mitral stenosis
- Enlarged aorta → aortic stenosis or aneurysm
- Neither → hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, restrictive physiology, acute MI, pulmonary hypertension

**Chamber clues**
- RV enlargement: leftward apex displacement, fills retrosternal clear space on lateral
- LV enlargement: left-inferior apex displacement

### CAD-RADS (Coronary Artery Disease Reporting and Data System)
| Category | Stenosis | Management notes |
|----------|----------|------------------|
| **0** | 0% | No plaque |
| **1** | 1–24% | Minimal |
| **2** | 25–49% | Mild |
| **3** | 50–69% | Moderate |
| **4** | 70–99% (or left main ≥50%) | Severe |
| **5** | 100% | Occluded |
| **N** | Nondiagnostic | Cannot exclude significant disease |

Always report the highest grade stenosis and any high-risk plaque features.

### Coronary Anomalies – Malignant vs Benign
**Malignant (interarterial course)**
- Anomalous coronary arising from the opposite sinus and coursing between aorta and pulmonary artery → risk of sudden death
- Anomalous origin from pulmonary artery (ALCAPA / ARCAPA)

**Other important**
- Coronary fistula
- Myocardial bridging (usually benign but can be symptomatic)

## 2. Cardiac MRI – Late Gadolinium Enhancement (LGE) Patterns

| Pattern | Typical Etiology | Key Features |
|---------|------------------|--------------|
| **Subendocardial or transmural** | Ischemic (CAD / infarct) | Follows coronary territory |
| **Mid-wall** | Non-ischemic cardiomyopathy (dilated, myocarditis) | Does not follow coronary territory |
| **Epicardial / subepicardial** | Myocarditis, sarcoid | Often patchy |
| **Global subendocardial** | Amyloidosis, cardiac transplant rejection | “Zebra” or diffuse |
| **RV insertion points** | Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, pulmonary hypertension | |

**Ischemic vs Non-ischemic rule**  
If LGE is subendocardial/transmural and matches a coronary territory → ischemic.  
Any other pattern → consider non-ischemic.

## 3. Cardiomyopathy Framework

**Dilated**  
- Enlarged LV + reduced EF  
- LGE mid-wall or none  
- Differential: idiopathic, toxic, viral, ischemic end-stage

**Hypertrophic (HCM)**  
- Asymmetric septal hypertrophy most common  
- LGE at RV insertion points or patchy mid-wall  
- Risk stratification for sudden death (thickness, LGE extent, family history)

**Restrictive**  
- Normal or small ventricles, biatrial enlargement  
- Amyloid (global subendocardial LGE), sarcoid, hemochromatosis, endomyocardial fibrosis

**Arrhythmogenic RV cardiomyopathy (ARVC)**  
- RV dilation/dysfunction, fatty replacement, LGE in RV  

**Myocarditis**  
- Epicardial/mid-wall LGE, T2 edema, often viral or autoimmune

## 4. Coronary CTA & Calcium Scoring

**Calcium score (Agatston)**
- Risk stratification in asymptomatic patients
- 0 = very low risk; higher scores correlate with plaque burden

**High-risk plaque features on CCTA**
- Positive remodeling
- Low-attenuation plaque
- Spotty calcification
- Napkin-ring sign

**Bypass grafts**  
CCTA accurately evaluates graft patency, but metallic clips can cause artifact.

## 5. Classic Signs & Patterns

| Sign / Pattern              | Meaning                                      | Importance |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------|
| **Hyperdense MCA equivalent (coronary)** | Acute coronary thrombus                 | Rare but specific |
| **Napkin-ring sign**        | High-risk plaque                             | Vulnerable plaque |
| **Double density (LA)**     | Left atrial enlargement on CXR               | Mitral disease |
| **Boot-shaped heart**       | Tetralogy of Fallot                          | Congenital |
| **Water-bottle heart**      | Large pericardial effusion                   | Classic CXR appearance |
| **Zebra / global subendo LGE** | Cardiac amyloidosis                       | Restrictive pattern |

## 6. Key Emergencies & Red Flags

- Interarterial anomalous coronary → risk of sudden cardiac death (especially in young athletes)
- Acute aortic syndrome overlapping with coronary (dissection involving coronary ostia)
- Cardiac tamponade (pericardial effusion with right heart chamber collapse)
- Myocardial rupture / contained rupture post-infarct
- Active cardiac sarcoidosis (arrhythmia risk)

---

## 7. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Do not call every mid-wall LGE “myocarditis” — consider dilated cardiomyopathy and sarcoid.
- Subendocardial LGE that does **not** follow a coronary territory is unusual for pure ischemic disease.
- Calcium score of zero does not completely exclude soft plaque in symptomatic patients.
- Interarterial course is the key malignant feature of anomalous coronaries — slit-like orifice and intramural segment add risk.
- “Water-bottle” heart is classic but not specific; always correlate with clinical tamponade physiology.
- CAD-RADS category is driven by the worst stenosis; always state it clearly.
