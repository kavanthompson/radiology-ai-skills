# Core Radiology – Gastrointestinal Imaging

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Ellen X. Sun, Junzi Shi, Jacob C. Mandell), Cambridge University Press, 2021. Chapter 2 – Gastrointestinal Imaging.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks and pattern recognition tools for residents. Not a summary — a working toolkit for acute abdomen, ABR Core, and daily reporting.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Acute Abdomen Decision Framework
When interpreting CT abdomen/pelvis for acute pain, systematically evaluate:
1. **Bowel** – obstruction, ischemia, inflammation, perforation
2. **Solid organs** – liver, spleen, pancreas, kidneys
3. **Vessels** – mesenteric ischemia, aneurysm, thrombosis
4. **Peritoneum / free air / free fluid**
5. **Appendix / diverticula / gallbladder**

### Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO) vs Ileus
- **Transition point** present → mechanical obstruction
- No transition point + diffusely dilated loops → adynamic ileus
- Closed-loop obstruction (C-shaped or U-shaped loop, radial vessels, whirl sign) = surgical emergency (high risk of ischemia/necrosis)

### Large Bowel Obstruction Key Causes
- Colon cancer (most common in adults)
- Volvulus (sigmoid > cecal)
- Diverticulitis with stricture
- Fecal impaction / Ogilvie (pseudo-obstruction)

### Appendicitis Spectrum
- Tip appendicitis → full appendicitis → perforation → abscess
- Key criteria: diameter >6 mm, wall thickening, periappendiceal fat stranding, appendicolith (highly specific)
- Mimics: epiploic appendagitis, omental infarction, terminal ileitis, cecal diverticulitis, pelvic inflammatory disease

## 2. High-Yield Classic Signs & Patterns

| Sign / Pattern              | Meaning                                      | Clinical Importance                     |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| **Whirl sign**              | Twisted mesenteric vessels                   | Closed-loop obstruction / volvulus      |
| **Target sign**             | Concentric rings of bowel wall               | Intussusception or severe edema         |
| **String of pearls**        | Small amounts of gas in dilated fluid-filled loops | SBO on radiograph                    |
| **Coffee bean sign**        | Sigmoid volvulus on radiograph               | Large bowel obstruction                 |
| **Beaked appearance**       | Tapered transition in volvulus               | Closed-loop risk                        |
| **Fat halo / comb sign**    | Mesenteric vascular engorgement              | Active Crohn’s disease                  |
| **Skip lesions**            | Discontinuous bowel involvement              | Crohn’s (vs continuous in UC)           |
| **Lead pipe colon**         | Featureless, rigid colon                     | Chronic ulcerative colitis              |
| **Thumbprinting**           | Mucosal edema / hemorrhage                   | Ischemia or severe colitis              |

## 3. Inflammatory Bowel Disease

**Crohn’s Disease**
- Any part of GI tract (terminal ileum classic)
- Transmural, skip lesions, fistulas, abscesses, strictures
- Comb sign, mesenteric fat proliferation (“creeping fat”)
- Perianal disease common

**Ulcerative Colitis**
- Continuous from rectum proximal
- Mucosal/submucosal only
- Lead-pipe appearance in chronic disease
- Strong association with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)

## 4. Pancreatitis Framework

**Role of Imaging**
- Ultrasound first-line for gallstones / biliary obstruction
- CT (pancreatic phase ~40 s) best for necrosis, collections, complications
- MRI/MRCP for ductal anatomy and fluid characterization

**Revised Atlanta Classification (key collections)**
- Acute peripancreatic fluid collection (APFC) – <4 weeks, no necrosis
- Acute necrotic collection (ANC) – <4 weeks, with necrosis
- Pseudocyst – ≥4 weeks, no necrosis
- Walled-off necrosis (WON) – ≥4 weeks, with necrosis

**Severity clues**: necrosis, gas (infection), vascular complications (pseudoaneurysm, thrombosis), extrapancreatic collections

## 5. Liver & Biliary High-Yield

**Cirrhosis Morphology**
- Right lobe atrophy + left lobe / caudate hypertrophy
- Nodular contour, widened fissures, right posterior hepatic notch
- Secondary signs of portal hypertension: varices, splenomegaly, ascites, recanalized paraumbilical vein, Gamna-Gandy bodies

**Key Biliary Entities**
- **PSC**: beaded irregular ducts, associated with UC, risk of cholangiocarcinoma
- **PBC**: middle-aged women, smaller ducts, pruritus
- **AIDS cholangiopathy**: looks like PSC + papillary stenosis
- **Recurrent pyogenic cholangitis**: pigment stones, SE Asian patients, Clonorchis association

**Liver Abscess Patterns**
- Pyogenic: often solitary or clustered, ring-enhancing
- Fungal (Candida): innumerable tiny microabscesses in immunocompromised
- Amebic: usually solitary, right lobe, travel history

---

## 6. Diverticulitis & Related

- Most common left-sided (sigmoid)
- Wall thickening + pericolonic fat stranding ± diverticula
- Complications: abscess, perforation, fistula, obstruction, pylephlebitis
- Right-sided diverticulitis can mimic appendicitis

**Epiploic appendagitis** – self-limited; oval fat-density lesion with hyperdense rim and central dot (central vessel)

---

## 7. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Do not call “ileus” if a clear transition point exists.
- Closed-loop obstruction is a surgical emergency — look for whirl, C/U shape, and signs of ischemia (reduced enhancement, pneumatosis, portal venous gas).
- Appendicitis can be tip-only — measure the entire appendix.
- Skip lesions + transmural disease = Crohn’s until proven otherwise.
- Beaded bile ducts + UC history = PSC (screen for cholangiocarcinoma).
- Pancreatic fluid collection age and presence of necrosis determine the Atlanta category — do not call everything a “pseudocyst.”
- Hypervascular liver lesions are best seen in late arterial phase; hypovascular metastases in portal venous phase.
