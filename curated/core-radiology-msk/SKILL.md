# Core Radiology – Musculoskeletal Imaging

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Ellen X. Sun, Junzi Shi, Jacob C. Mandell), Cambridge University Press, 2021. Chapter 13 – Musculoskeletal Imaging.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks for residents. Working toolkit for arthritis, trauma, infection, and bone/soft-tissue tumors.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Arthritis Pattern Recognition
**Osteoarthritis (OA)**
- Weight-bearing joints + hands (DIP > PIP > CMC of thumb)
- Osteophytes, joint-space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, cysts
- Heberden nodes (DIP soft-tissue swelling), Bouchard nodes (PIP)
- MCPs relatively spared (unlike RA)

**Erosive OA**
- Elderly females, hands only
- Gull-wing appearance at DIP (central erosion + marginal osteophytes)
- Combines clinical swelling of RA with OA distribution

**Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)**
- Symmetric, proximal (MCP, PIP, wrists, MTP)
- Marginal erosions, periarticular osteopenia, uniform joint-space narrowing
- No osteophytes (until secondary OA)

**Seronegative Spondyloarthropathies**
- Asymmetric, distal, enthesitis, sacroiliitis
- Ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic, reactive, IBD-related

### Fracture Terminology
- **Direction**: transverse, oblique, spiral
- **Displacement**: displaced, foreshortened, distracted
- **Angulation**: valgus (distal fragment points laterally), varus (medially)
- **Comminuted special types**: butterfly, segmental
- **Salter-Harris** (pediatric physis): I–V (II most common)

### Stress Fracture Spectrum
- **Fatigue fracture**: normal bone + repetitive abnormal force (runners, military)
- **Insufficiency fracture**: abnormal bone + normal force (osteoporosis, osteomalacia)
- **Pathologic fracture**: underlying lesion (tumor, infection)
- **Atypical fracture**: bisphosphonate-related (lateral femoral cortex, transverse)

## 2. Infection Frameworks

### Osteomyelitis MRI
- **Confluent T1 hypointense marrow signal** is the strongest predictor of osteomyelitis
- Hazy reticular or subcortical signal is less specific
- Gadolinium helps define abscess and nonenhancing sequestrum but is not required for diagnosis

### Soft-Tissue Infection
- **Necrotizing fasciitis**: gas in soft tissues on XR/CT → surgical emergency
- Cellulitis vs abscess: ultrasound or MRI for fluid collections

### Discitis-Osteomyelitis
- Endplate destruction, disc T2 hyperintensity + enhancement, paraspinal/epidural phlegmon or abscess

## 3. Bone Tumor Approach

### Matrix Patterns
- **Osteoid**: cloud-like, ivory, or solid dense bone
- **Chondroid**: arcs and rings, punctate calcifications
- **Fibrous**: ground-glass
- **Lytic / aggressive**: permeative, moth-eaten, cortical destruction

### Age + Location + Margins
- Young + epiphyseal + geographic → chondroblastoma
- Young + metaphyseal + eccentric → non-ossifying fibroma / ABC
- Adult + diaphyseal + permeative → Ewing or lymphoma
- Adult + epiphyseal/metaphyseal + geographic → giant cell tumor (after physeal closure)

### Classic “Don’t Touch” or Characteristic Lesions
- Non-ossifying fibroma / fibrous cortical defect
- Bone island (enostosis)
- Osteochondroma (continuity of cortex and medullary cavity)
- Enchondroma (arcs and rings in short bones of hands/feet)

---

## 4. Soft-Tissue Tumors High-Yield

- Most common benign: lipoma
- Most common malignant in adults: undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma / liposarcoma
- **MRI signal**: fat-containing (lipoma, well-differentiated liposarcoma), myxoid (T2 bright), hemorrhage, enhancement patterns
- Biopsy for indeterminate or aggressive features

## 5. Classic Signs & Patterns

| Sign / Pattern              | Meaning                                      | Importance |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------|
| **Gull-wing**               | Central erosion + marginal osteophytes at DIP | Erosive OA |
| **Heberden / Bouchard**     | Soft-tissue swelling at DIP / PIP            | OA of hands |
| **Double density**          | Osteoid osteoma on bone scan                 | Nidus + reactive bone |
| **Fallen fragment**         | Pathologic fracture through unicameral bone cyst | Benign cystic lesion |
| **Codman triangle**         | Periosteal reaction from rapid periosteal elevation | Aggressive tumor or infection |
| **Onion-skin / sunburst**   | Aggressive periosteal reaction               | Ewing / osteosarcoma |

## 6. Joint-Specific High-Yield

**Shoulder**
- Grashey view profiles the glenohumeral joint best for OA
- Rotator cuff, labrum, biceps on MRI

**Knee**
- OA predominantly medial compartment
- Meniscus, ACL, PCL, cartilage on MRI

**Hip**
- OA, AVN (double-line sign), FAI morphology
- Insufficiency fractures of femoral neck / sacral ala

**Ankle / Foot**
- Great toe MTP most common OA site (hallux rigidus)
- Lisfranc, Jones, stress fractures of metatarsals

---

## 7. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Do not call every DIP swelling “RA” — check distribution and presence of osteophytes (OA).
- Gull-wing = erosive OA, not RA.
- Confluent T1 marrow replacement is required for confident osteomyelitis diagnosis on MRI.
- Gas in soft tissues = necrotizing fasciitis until proven otherwise (surgical emergency).
- Stress fractures can be occult on radiographs — MRI or bone scan when clinical suspicion is high.
- “Atypical fracture” has a specific meaning (bisphosphonate-related lateral femoral cortex).
- Always assess for soft-tissue mass or cortical breakthrough when evaluating an aggressive bone lesion.
