# Core Radiology – Interventional Radiology

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Ellen X. Sun, Junzi Shi, Jacob C. Mandell), Cambridge University Press, 2021. Chapter 9 – Interventional Radiology.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks for residents. Working toolkit for procedural safety, embolization, TIPS, drainage, biopsies, and oncologic interventions.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Pre-Procedure Essentials
- Directed H&P, risks/benefits discussion, formal consent
- Anesthesia options: local → moderate sedation (midazolam/fentanyl) → MAC / general
- Fasting: typically NPO 6–8 hours for solids, 2 hours for clears (varies by sedation level)
- Antibiotic prophylaxis for selected procedures (biliary, GU, abscess, prosthetic material)

### Bleeding Risk Stratification (SIR Guidelines Style)
**Low bleeding risk**  
INR <2.0–3.0, platelets >20 × 10⁹/L  
Examples: IVC filter placement/removal, venous access, thoracentesis/paracentesis, superficial biopsy/drainage, lumbar puncture

**High bleeding risk**  
INR <1.5–1.8, platelets >50 × 10⁹/L  
Examples: arterial interventions (>7 Fr), deep solid-organ biopsy/drainage, nephrostomy, cholecystostomy, gastrostomy, TIPS, complex filter removal, ablations, catheter-directed thrombolysis

**Reversal agents**
- Heparin/enoxaparin → protamine
- Warfarin → vitamin K + FFP or PCC (Kcentra)
- Factor Xa inhibitors → andexanet alfa (when available)

### Embolization Principles
- **Proximal vs distal**: distal for organ preservation when collaterals exist; proximal for rapid control or when distal access is impossible
- Particle size selection based on target vessel caliber and desired permanence
- Coil embolization for larger vessels or when precise deployment needed
- Liquid agents (glue, Onyx, alcohol) for permanent occlusion or complex anatomy
- Always consider non-target embolization risk (especially cerebral, spinal, GI)

## 2. Key Arterial Interventions

**Trauma / hemorrhage**
- Embolize as selectively as possible while achieving hemostasis
- Temporary agents (Gelfoam) for temporary control; permanent for definitive

**Oncologic**
- Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) / radioembolization (Y-90) for HCC and selected metastases
- Pre-embolization mapping to identify extrahepatic feeders and avoid non-target (GI, gallbladder, lung)

**Uterine fibroid embolization (UFE)**
- Bilateral uterine artery embolization
- Particle size typically 500–700 µm or larger to spare normal tissue

**Prostate artery embolization (PAE)**
- Growing indication for BPH; careful identification of prostatic arteries and anastomoses

## 3. Venous & Portal Interventions

### TIPS (Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt)
**Indications**
- Refractory variceal hemorrhage
- Refractory ascites
- Budd-Chiari (selected)

**Absolute contraindications**
- Severe right heart failure
- Severe uncontrolled hepatic encephalopathy
- Severe active hepatic failure / high MELD (relative in many centers)

**Technical notes**
- Create shunt from hepatic vein to portal vein
- Target portosystemic gradient reduction (usually <12 mmHg)
- Follow-up with Doppler ultrasound for patency

**BRTO / BATO**  
Balloon-occluded retrograde transvenous obliteration for gastric varices (especially when TIPS is contraindicated or as alternative)

### IVC Filters
- Indications: contraindication to anticoagulation + acute VTE, or failure of anticoagulation
- Retrieval as soon as clinically feasible to reduce long-term complications (fracture, migration, IVC penetration)

## 4. Drainage, Biopsy & Access

**Abscess drainage**
- Prefer percutaneous when accessible and no intervening vital structures
- Catheter size matched to viscosity of contents
- Always send fluid for culture + consider cytology if malignancy possible

**Biliary / GU drainage**
- Percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD) and percutaneous nephrostomy
- High bleeding risk; correct coagulopathy
- External vs internal-external vs internalized stents

**Biopsy principles**
- Coaxial technique when multiple samples or ablation planned
- Tract embolization for high-risk sites (liver, kidney, lung)
- On-site cytology when available for adequacy

## 5. Classic Signs, Numbers & Red Flags

| Item                        | Key Point                                    | Clinical Importance |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|---------------------|
| **MELD score**              | Higher = higher post-TIPS mortality          | Risk stratification |
| **Portosystemic gradient**  | Target <12 mmHg after TIPS                   | Technical success |
| **Non-target embolization** | Especially cystic artery, GI, spinal         | Major complication |
| **Filter retrieval window** | Ideally within weeks to months               | Reduce long-term risk |
| **Coagulopathy thresholds** | High-risk procedures need tighter control    | Prevent hemorrhage |

---

## 6. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Do not place TIPS in severe right heart failure or uncontrolled encephalopathy.
- Never embolize more proximally than necessary when collaterals can be preserved.
- IVC filters are not permanent devices by default — plan for retrieval.
- High bleeding risk procedures require stricter coagulation parameters than low-risk ones.
- Always map for extrahepatic feeders before Y-90 or TACE to avoid GI ulceration or cholecystitis.
- Particle size too small → increased risk of non-target and tissue necrosis beyond intended zone.
