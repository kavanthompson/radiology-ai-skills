# Core Radiology – Breast Imaging

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition (Ellen X. Sun, Junzi Shi, Jacob C. Mandell), Cambridge University Press, 2021. Chapter 5 – Breast Imaging.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks for residents. Working toolkit for BI-RADS, calcifications, masses, asymmetries, and multimodal breast imaging.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### BI-RADS Assessment Categories (5th Edition)
| Category | Meaning | Typical Action |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| **0** | Incomplete – need additional imaging / priors | Recall for diagnostic workup (screening only) |
| **1** | Negative | Routine screening |
| **2** | Benign | Routine screening |
| **3** | Probably benign (≤2% malignancy) | Short-interval follow-up (6 months) |
| **4** | Suspicious (A low / B moderate / C high) | Biopsy |
| **5** | Highly suggestive of malignancy (≥95%) | Biopsy |
| **6** | Known biopsy-proven malignancy | Surgical planning / neoadjuvant |

**Screening mammograms** can only be BI-RADS 0, 1, or 2.  
**Diagnostic** studies receive final categories 1–6.

### Breast Composition (Density)
1. Almost entirely fatty  
2. Scattered areas of fibroglandular density  
3. Heterogeneously dense (may obscure small masses)  
4. Extremely dense (lowers sensitivity)

### Probably Benign (BI-RADS 3) – Strict Criteria
Only three evidence-based scenarios on baseline/diagnostic imaging:
1. Noncalcified circumscribed solid mass (after full workup including ultrasound)
2. Focal asymmetry that becomes less conspicuous on diagnostic views or is a summation artifact
3. Cluster of pure punctate calcifications (must be clearly punctate)

Anything outside these criteria should not be called BI-RADS 3.

## 2. Calcifications – Morphology & Distribution

### Morphology (from least to most suspicious)
- **Typically benign**: skin, vascular, coarse (“popcorn”), large rod-like, round, rim, dystrophic, milk of calcium, suture
- **Intermediate**: amorphous, coarse heterogeneous
- **Higher probability of malignancy**: fine pleomorphic, fine linear / fine linear branching

### Distribution
- Diffuse / scattered → usually benign
- Regional → intermediate
- Grouped / clustered (<2 cm) → concerning if suspicious morphology
- Linear / segmental → highest suspicion (ductal distribution)

**Rule of thumb**: Fine linear branching in a segmental distribution is classic for DCIS.

---

## 3. Masses & Asymmetries

### Mass Descriptors
- **Shape**: oval, round, irregular
- **Margins**: circumscribed, obscured, microlobulated, indistinct, spiculated
- **Density**: high, equal, low, fat-containing

Spiculated + high density = highly suspicious.

### Asymmetry Types
- **Asymmetry**: one-view finding
- **Global asymmetry**: large volume of tissue relative to contralateral breast
- **Focal asymmetry**: two-view, concave margins, interspersed fat
- **Developing asymmetry**: new or increasing compared to prior → higher risk, requires workup

Developing asymmetry is never BI-RADS 3 on a baseline exam if suspicious features are present.

## 4. Cancer Subtypes – Imaging Pearls

**Invasive Ductal Carcinoma (IDC)**  
Most common. Classic spiculated mass ± calcifications.

**Invasive Lobular Carcinoma (ILC)**  
5–10% of cases. Subtle: one-view asymmetry, architectural distortion, or shrinking breast. Harder to detect mammographically.

**Inflammatory Carcinoma**  
Dermal lymphatic invasion. Clinical peau d’orange + diffuse skin/trabecular thickening. Differential includes mastitis.

**Paget Disease of the Nipple**  
Eczematous nipple changes ± underlying DCIS or invasive cancer.

**DCIS**  
Most often presents as calcifications (fine linear branching / segmental).

## 5. Multimodality Pearls

**Ultrasound**
- First-line for palpable abnormalities, young patients (<30), and further characterization of mammographic findings
- Simple cyst = BI-RADS 2
- Complex cystic/solid → biopsy

**MRI**
- High sensitivity; used for high-risk screening, extent of disease, occult primary, problem-solving
- Kinetic curves: persistent (type 1), plateau (type 2), washout (type 3 – most suspicious)
- Background parenchymal enhancement (BPE) must be described

**Digital Breast Tomosynthesis (DBT)**
- Decreases recall rates and increases cancer detection compared with 2D mammography alone

## 6. Classic Signs & Patterns

| Sign / Pattern              | Meaning                                      | Importance |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------|
| **Fine linear branching**   | DCIS                                         | High suspicion morphology |
| **Segmental distribution**  | Ductal process                               | Raises concern with suspicious morphology |
| **Spiculated mass**         | Invasive cancer until proven otherwise       | BI-RADS 4C/5 |
| **Architectural distortion**| Possible malignancy or radial scar           | Needs workup |
| **Peau d’orange**           | Inflammatory carcinoma or mastitis           | Clinical correlation critical |
| **Developing asymmetry**    | New or increasing density                    | Higher risk – full workup |

## 7. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Do not assign BI-RADS 3 outside the three evidence-based scenarios.
- Screening exams cannot be BI-RADS 3, 4, or 5 — use 0 for incomplete.
- ILC is frequently subtle; do not dismiss one-view findings without careful comparison.
- Inflammatory carcinoma and mastitis can look identical on imaging — clinical context decides.
- Amorphous or coarse heterogeneous calcifications in a grouped distribution still warrant biopsy (BI-RADS 4).
- “Probably benign” means ≤2% chance of malignancy — short-interval follow-up is mandatory, not optional.
