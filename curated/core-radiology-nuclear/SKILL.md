# Core Radiology – Nuclear Medicine

**Source**: *Core Radiology: A Visual Approach to Diagnostic Imaging*, 2nd Edition + ABR Core high-yield Nuclear Medicine topics.

**Purpose**: Actionable frameworks for residents. Working toolkit for V/Q, bone scans, thyroid, PET, and GI bleeding studies.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### V/Q Scintigraphy (PIOPED / Modified PIOPED II)
- **High probability**: ≥2 large mismatched segmental perfusion defects
- **Intermediate**: mixed or difficult-to-categorize findings
- **Low / very low**: matched defects, non-segmental, triple-matched in lower lung zones, etc.
- **Normal**: no perfusion defects

Always correlate with chest radiograph. Triple-match in upper/mid lung can be intermediate; lower lung triple-match more likely low probability.

### Bone Scintigraphy Patterns
- **Superscan**: diffuse intense skeletal uptake + absent or faint renal/soft-tissue activity (metastatic prostate, hyperparathyroidism)
- **Three-phase bone scan**: positive in osteomyelitis, fracture, tumor (flow, blood pool, delayed)
- **Flare phenomenon**: temporary worsening after therapy initiation
- **Metastases**: random axial + proximal appendicular distribution; “cold” lesions possible (aggressive, anaplastic)

### Thyroid Scintigraphy
- **Hot nodule**: almost never malignant (autonomous adenoma)
- **Cold nodule**: higher risk of malignancy (further evaluate with US ± FNA)
- **Graves**: diffuse increased uptake
- **Thyroiditis**: low uptake in destructive phase
- **Ectopic thyroid / lingual thyroid**: search with Tc-99m or I-123

### PET/CT (FDG)
- **SUV** is semi-quantitative; compare to background and prior
- Brown fat, infection, inflammation, recent surgery/radiation can cause false positives
- Low-level uptake in some malignancies (well-differentiated, mucinous, some prostate)

## 2. Key Radiopharmaceuticals

| Study                  | Tracer              | Critical Notes |
|------------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Bone scan              | Tc-99m MDP / HDP    | Uptake depends on osteoblastic activity + blood flow |
| V/Q                    | Tc-99m MAA (perf) + Xe/Tc-DTPA (vent) | Particle size critical for MAA |
| Thyroid                | I-123 or Tc-99m     | I-131 for therapy |
| GI bleed               | Tc-99m RBC          | Active bleeding localization |
| Meckel’s               | Tc-99m pertechnetate| Focal uptake in RLQ |
| PET oncology           | F-18 FDG            | Glucose analog |
| Cardiac perfusion      | Tc-99m sestamibi / tetrofosmin or Tl-201 | |

## 3. Classic Patterns & Signs

| Pattern                     | Meaning                                      |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| **Superscan**               | Diffuse bone metastases or metabolic disease |
| **Mismatched perfusion defect** | PE until proven otherwise                |
| **Triple match**            | Intermediate or low probability depending on zone |
| **Hot nose + cold kidneys** | Brain death (or technical)                   |
| **Rim sign (GB)**           | Acute cholecystitis (pericholecystic hepatic uptake) |
| **Flare**                   | Treatment response, not progression          |

## 4. Anti-Patterns & Core Exam Traps

- Do not call every matched V/Q defect “low probability” without considering size, number, and chest x-ray correlation.
- Superscan can look “normal” if you forget to notice the absent kidney activity.
- Cold thyroid nodules still need workup; hot nodules are reassuring but not 100% benign in all contexts.
- FDG avidity ≠ cancer; infection and inflammation are common mimics.
- Free technetium (poor labeling) causes stomach, thyroid, and choroid plexus uptake on bone or RBC scans.
